---
name: Product positioning and monthly-first offering
description: Why the product differs from ancestry tools, and how the user wants the offer presented.
---

Position OurLifeCapsule as a place and a structure to build a memory capsule, either physical or digital: "we do both". The user contrasted this with family trees and ancestry research, which do not provide that memory-capsule structure. Do not describe it as an ancestry-research service.

**Why:** The user wants people to understand the value and what the company does within the first 10 seconds of entering the site.

**How to apply:** Explain the concrete activity and result near the top: gather photographs, voices, letters and stories using guided tools; create a digital capsule, a physical keepsake, or both. Clearly distinguish separately priced physical products from digital membership.

Lead with genuine monthly pricing rather than a monthly equivalent that requires an annual payment. Keep annual billing optional and disclose its full total. The user chose lower test launch prices rather than retaining the initial prices.

**Why:** The user felt roughly $100 a year looked expensive at face value and that monthly pricing would be more enticing.

**How to apply:** Keep monthly selected by default on pricing surfaces and retain clear demo-only pricing and disconnected-service disclosures. Do not treat these launch hypotheses as validated willingness to pay.
